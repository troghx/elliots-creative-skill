# Kenji Lambert — observed 2026-11-09

Source: https://www.kenjilambert.com/ (portfolio of a designer and art director, v2.0). These are observations of the public homepage and the devices behind it, translated into reusable ideas. The work, illustrations, copy, mascot and identity belong to their author: never reproduce them.

## What makes it work

The site feels like a person, not a template. A few strong devices carry it, and every one serves the content.

## Devices and how to translate them

**1. Objects inside the sentence.** The headline is a phrase with parenthesis-wrapped slots where small illustrated objects appear and swap (a birdcage, headphones, a box, a drum).
- Transfer: put the brand's own objects into the statement itself (a product's ingredients, a tool's file types, a studio's instruments). The sentence stays readable as text; the objects add warmth.
- Rules: the real text must be in the DOM and readable without the images; swaps pause under reduced motion; no more than two slots per sentence.

**2. Four-corner navigation.** Logo top left, a main link top right, two more bottom left and right, language toggle top center, all fixed. The content scrolls through the frame.
- Transfer: portfolios and campaign sites with a few destinations. It frees the whole canvas for content.
- Rules: corners need sufficient contrast over every section; keep a normal, ordered nav for keyboard and screen readers.

**3. A hanging object with physics.** A card hangs from a cord, like a badge or tag, and moves with the page.
- Transfer: a name tag, ticket or price tag that reacts to scroll or pointer, once per page. It works as a signature moment.
- Rules: never in front of text people must read; still under reduced motion; never on a frequent-use tool.

**4. Text that comes into focus.** The bio starts blurred and sharpens word by word as it scrolls into view, with key phrases color-coded and one word set in a pill.
- Transfer: one introductory paragraph per page, where reading order matters.
- Rules: the text is already readable when it lands; do not block reading on scroll position; skip under reduced motion.

**5. A tiny, loud palette.** Near-black ground, cream text, one tomato red and one cornflower blue. The two accents alternate across highlighted words and names, so the rhythm itself becomes the identity.
- Transfer: two accents with a clear rule (alternation, or a fixed meaning each) beat five decorative ones. Check contrast of each accent on the ground.

**6. Three type voices.** A condensed, heavy display face for statements, a clean grotesk for reading, and an italic serif for the crafts ("motion, 3D, illustration").
- Transfer: give each voice one job; the accent face appears only on the words it dignifies.

**7. Giant marquee dividers.** Section titles ("FEATURED WORKS ✱") run edge to edge as moving bands, with a repeated glyph as punctuation. The footer turns the email address into the same band.
- Transfer: one recurring glyph as brand punctuation; a marquee as a section boundary, never as a replacement for hierarchy.
- Rules: provide the static title for assistive tech; pause on hover and under reduced motion.

**8. Case rows instead of a card grid.** Each project row carries discipline pills, a short condensed title, year and client, a two-line summary, a "see full case ↗" pill, and a horizontal strip of large images below.
- Transfer: portfolios, case studies, product changelogs, release notes. It reads faster than a grid and gives images room.

**9. Logos with icons in a slow band.** Client names with small marks move in a quiet line beneath the bio.
- Transfer: only with real clients the user names; never invent logos.

## Where it fits

Strong: portfolios, personal brands, studios, campaigns, launch pages, about pages, 404 and empty states. Moderate: a product landing's hero or story section. Poor: anything used all day. There the playful devices become friction; borrow the palette discipline and type voices instead.

## Do not copy

The phrase "making things is the job", the mascot, the hanging card's artwork, the illustrations, case content, client lists or the exact color values as a set. Build original devices in the same spirit.
